You wake in a dark, unfamiliar alley with no memory of how you arrived. The space seems sentient: bricks hinge into doors where walls once stood, shadows whisper, stray cats hesitate at thresholds, and sun-washed graffiti hums with borrowed memories. Scattered relics—a pocket watch, a diary page, a cracked magnifying glass—carry voices that nudge (or mislead) you forward. Every corner is a choice, and every choice branches the narrative, subtly reconfiguring the alley and what it’s willing to reveal. As you explore, you assemble a portrait of your forgotten past—one that shifts depending on what you heed, what you ignore, and what you’re ready to remember. Cipher Alley is a compact, replayable mystery about memory, perspective, and how the stories we choose become the walls around us.
